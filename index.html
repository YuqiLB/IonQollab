BEHIND THE SCENE

What actually ran?

Real classical mathematics. Your text becomes UTF-8 bytes, each stored as three base-15 digits. Each digit is encrypted with tiny, unpadded textbook RSA. After a verified period yields factors, the recovered exponent decrypts the actual packet.

Quantum execution. Illustrative mode uses labeled deterministic sample measurements. Live mode requires a Python connection and your Qollab-selected simulator. A failed live job never becomes sample success. The circuit drawing is an order-finding schematic, not a backend circuit trace.

Post-quantum exchange. The ML-KEM, key derivation, and authenticated-encryption sequence is an illustration. No actual ML-KEM or authenticated encryption runs here. Both messages stay in this browser’s memory; there is no secure chat service.

The story assumes a future sufficiently powerful quantum machine. This does not demonstrate breaking AES, a production messaging service, or all end-to-end encryption. Tiny RSA is also trivial to break classically. Here e = d = 3, so even the public exponent decrypts; factoring is shown to teach the recovery chain. ML-KEM is designed to resist known quantum attacks; it is not a claim of invulnerability. Public keys must also be authenticated in a real protocol.

Homomorphic encryption means computation on encrypted data; it is a different concept from post-quantum cryptography.
